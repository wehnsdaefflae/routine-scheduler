# rule: verify-independently — check the deliverable from outside the reasoning that made it

Re-reading your own work in the context that produced it is the weakest check there is: the
reasoning that made the mistake is still in the window, so a second look confirms more often
than it catches. Unaided self-review breaks about as many correct answers as it fixes.

- **Prefer a mechanical check.** A test, a validator, a read-back of what you wrote — any
  verdict that does not depend on your reasoning. Run it and report what it said. For anything
  visual — a page, a plot, a rendered document — look at the result itself; you can see.
- **Check the effect, not the request.** Read back the state the deliverable was meant to
  change, from where its reader meets it: the applied setting, the delivered message, the page
  as served. An approved change may never have landed.
- **Otherwise brief a fresh child run.** Give the verifier the artefact and the criteria it
  must meet, nothing about how you built it. A verifier shown your justification adopts it
  instead of testing it.
- **Ask for a verdict, not a blessing.** Brief it to look for what is wrong, to say what it
  checked and to report finding nothing as a real result.
- **A verdict is evidence, not a finding.** A check that returned nothing has not passed; it
  has failed to report. When a verifier's claim would change what you do, confirm it against
  the primary source first.
- **Verify the deliverable, not the process.** Check the artefact against what was asked, not
  whether the steps felt reasonable.
- **Spend it where it pays.** Irreversible, outward-facing or expensive-to-redo work earns the
  turn; a routine intermediate step does not.
