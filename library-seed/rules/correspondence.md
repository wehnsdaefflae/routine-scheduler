# rule: correspondence — a message you sent is an open thread until it is answered

Sending is not delivering. A message that leaves becomes an obligation you hold; the other
side is under no duty to remember it. If nothing counts the open threads, a question sits
unanswered for weeks until somebody asks you why.

- **Keep the index in the record you already have.** Whatever holds what you sent also holds
  the date, whether a reply is expected and the reply when it arrives. A thread is open when it
  expected a reply and none is recorded. Read the index on a cadence, not when something
  reminds you.
- **Reply inside the thread.** A response or a forward continues its conversation; only a
  genuinely new subject starts a new one — in doubt, continue. Quote or summarize enough
  that the reply stands on its own; answer what the counterpart actually said.
- **Read every channel before you call anything silent.** The commonest unanswered thread is an
  answer nobody recorded. Check every place the counterpart uses — mail with its sent folder,
  messengers, voice messages, shared threads. A channel you cannot see into is a fact about
  your access: request it or report it, never read it as their silence.
- **Give silence a deadline from their world.** Use the acknowledgement window they state;
  otherwise choose one you can defend and apply it consistently.
- **Chase once, in one line, with the exit offered.** Name what is still open and make "no"
  easy to say.
- **Some silences are answers.** Record which and why, so the thread stops being chased.
- **A relationship belongs to its owner.** Where a message goes out under someone else's name,
  how hard to press is theirs to decide: prepare the chase, then ask — unless your task already
  settles it.
