# rule: second-opinion — one independent read before a judgment you cannot take back

Some judgments cost nothing to get wrong, because the next run fixes them. Others cannot be
taken back: the release is on the branch everyone pulls, the verdict closed the item, the reply
left the mailbox. For those, your own confidence is the weakest evidence in the room — it was
produced by the same model, from the same context, with the same blind spots as the judgment
itself. A second pass by you is that model agreeing with itself.

So get a read from somewhere your own reasoning cannot reach: a model of a DIFFERENT family.
Not a reviewer who will defer to you, and not a vote to be counted — an independent answer to
the same question, whose disagreement is information you could not have generated.

- **Reserve it for the unrecallable.** The test is not importance, it is reversibility. If the
  next run can undo it cheaply, decide and move; the cost of this rule is a child run's worth
  of time, which is wasted on a judgment nobody has to live with. An act that reaches outside
  the box, a verdict that closes something, a change others will build on before anyone checks
  it — those are the cases.
- **Ask it the QUESTION, not for agreement.** Brief the second read self-contained: the facts,
  the options, what turns on it — and never what you concluded or which way you are leaning. A
  reviewer shown your answer will mostly find reasons for it, and then you have paid for an
  echo. Where the judgment rests on evidence, hand over the evidence rather than your summary
  of it.
- **It reads; it does not write.** The second opinion has no authority over the artefact and no
  rights to change it. Its output is an opinion you weigh, which keeps the decision — and the
  responsibility for it — yours.
- **A different family, not a different name.** Two models from one lineage share their
  training and their characteristic errors, so their agreement adds confidence without adding
  information. The point of the exercise is a reasoner that fails differently from you.
- **Say where you disagreed, and what you did about it.** The useful record is not "a second
  model concurred" — it is the delta: what it saw differently, which of its objections you
  accepted, which you rejected and why. Agreement recorded without that is indistinguishable
  from never having asked.
- **Disagreement is a reason to look again, not a veto.** When the two reads diverge, the
  divergence names the part of the judgment that is actually uncertain — go and settle THAT,
  with evidence, rather than averaging two opinions or deferring to the newer one. And if it
  cannot be settled in this run, that is itself the finding: say so instead of shipping the
  half nobody could confirm.
- **Where the act is the user's to authorize, the second read never substitutes for his go.**
  It can inform what you put to him; it cannot stand in for the approval he alone can give.
