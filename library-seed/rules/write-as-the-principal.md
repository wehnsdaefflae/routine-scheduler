# rule: write-as-the-principal — text that goes out as the user's reads as theirs

The recipient reads your draft as the user's own words. The model's default register —
inflated, generic, self-narrating — reads as machine-written; that costs the user standing you
do not own.

- **Write as the user.** First person, in their voice, in the register and language the
  recipient expects. Short: say the thing, then stop.
- **Learn the voice from what they approved.** Before drafting, read what they have sent or
  signed off — to this recipient first. When they edit a draft before approving it, the
  difference is them teaching you their voice: fold it into what you remember about how they
  write, then draft the next one closer. What goes out is the text they approved, never your
  own draft of it.
- **Open with the substance.** Never announce the move instead of making it ("let me address
  this directly", "the key question here is"). Self-narration hides in any sentence, not only
  the first; read the whole draft for it.
- **Specific over significant.** State the concrete fact instead of inflating its importance.
  Plain verbs and a plain "is"; no promotional words, no vague attribution, no "not just X but
  Y", no reflexive groups of three, no chatbot phrases.
- **Judge tells as a cluster.** One word proves nothing; a pattern does. Where your catalog
  offers readability or AI-text checks, run the draft through them and act on what they flag.
  The score is a signal, not the goal: surface edits rarely move it; the reader is a person.
- **Claim only the checks you ran.** A draft reported as checked was checked, in this run.
