# rule: decision-record — keep the reasoning the artefacts cannot carry

What you produce shows what the state is. It cannot show what you tried and abandoned, what
you weighed or why the obvious approach was wrong — exactly what a later run needs so it does
not buy the same lesson twice. That record is your FINISH SUMMARY's `**Process:**` part: the
engine files every finished run's summary, accounting and recipe-revision lines into the
routine's LEDGER.md itself, rotates it, and every later run's digest carries the earlier
entries. You never write, edit or rotate LEDGER.md — the engine refuses it; what you would have
put there goes in the summary, or in a `note` on the action where you learned it.

- **Read the record before you explore.** A dead end already paid for is not worth
  rediscovering; an idea already rejected is not worth proposing again. Read LEDGER.md, or its
  older months under `.ledger/`, when the digest's entries do not settle it.
- **Say what you decided, in the summary you already write.** What changed and why, the
  decisions you took, the candidates you rejected with the reason for each. Negative evidence
  is the most valuable part and the first thing a hurried summary drops.
- **Write for a reader without your context.** Name the artefact, give the evidence, say what
  done looked like. A summary only you could decode records nothing.
- **Say which of it you measured.** The record is read as settled: a later run obeys what it
  finds there instead of re-deriving it, which is the point. So a summary has to let the reader
  tell an observation from a conclusion from an assumption — what you ran and what came back,
  what you inferred from it, what you merely supposed and never tested. Write a mechanism you
  did not test as the supposition it is, and name the check that would settle it. One run wrote
  an untested mechanism into its record in the same voice as a measurement; later runs obeyed it
  for days against what the artefact plainly did, and the operator met the result — *"a false
  belief a run wrote and then obeyed"* (`llmsectest-weekday:20261020-040003`). When a later run
  disproves such a claim, its own summary says so and names the entry it overturns, and any
  `.memory/` note repeating the claim is corrected, so nobody inherits it a third time.
- **A record at its size limit must still accept a correction — so SPLIT the topic, never
  condense the evidence.** A capped store (a `.memory/` note) refuses the write exactly when it
  matters most: the moment a run has disproved something it asserts. Condensing what is already
  established to make room trades proven evidence for an unproven claim, which is the wrong
  thing to lose. Move the topic into a note of its own and leave a pointer where it was, so the
  correction fits and nothing established is destroyed. Measured in
  `moltbook-heartbeat:20261021-164943`: two edits to a note sitting at its cap were refused while
  the block being patched asserted a mechanism that run had just refuted; splitting the topic
  out made the correction fit in one write. A limit was set to keep a record readable — it was
  never a licence to keep a known-false assertion because the true one does not fit.
- **Artefacts stay present-tense.** What a thing is belongs in the thing; how it got there
  belongs in the record.
